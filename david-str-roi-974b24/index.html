    What the numbers include

    • Income: the seller's reported gross when the listing states it (unverified, ask for 24 months of statements), otherwise the BNBCalc trailing 12 month average for that area and bedroom count (data Nov 3, 2026).
    • Costs taken out: manager 20% (also shown self managed; at 25% returns drop about 5 points of gross), cleaning per stay at about a 4 night average stay, HOA, property tax at the Hotel and Resort rate of $13.90 per $1,000 of assessed value, insurance, utilities when not in HOA, supplies, 5% reserves, registration and accounting.
    • Hawaii GET 4.712% plus TAT 11% plus Oahu TAT 3% (18.712% total) is charged to guests on top of the nightly rate and paid over to the state, so it is neither income nor cost here. If a seller's gross figure includes those taxes, the real income is about 16% lower.
    • Cash Return (Cap Rate) = yearly net income ÷ price. With all cash there is no loan, so cash on cash and cap rate are one number. Closing costs (about 1%) are not in it.
    • Appreciation is the yearly change in Oahu-wide prices, shown for 5 and 10 years: condos and hotel condos use the Honolulu Board of REALTORS® annual Oahu condo resale median ($360,000 in 2015, $435,000 in 2020, $507,250 in 2025: 3.1% a year over 5 years, 3.5% over 10); houses use the single family median ($700,000, $830,000, $1,139,000: 6.5% and 5.0%); apartment buildings use the FHFA all-transactions house price index for Urban Honolulu, Q4 2015 to Q4 2025 (6.2% and 4.6%), since the Board does not track multifamily. Area by area annual series are not published, so the same island figure applies to every listing of that type. Past appreciation does not predict future prices.
    • *Est. total return = Cash Return + 10 year yearly appreciation. It is an estimate for comparing properties, not a forecast.
    • Investor Score: each metric is ranked against the other properties on this page (0 = weakest, 100 = strongest) and weighted: Cash Return 35, est. total return 20, monthly net cash flow 10, gross rent multiplier 10 (lower better), expense ratio 10 (lower better, includes the manager), 10 year appreciation 10, price per unit 5 (lower better; a condo counts as one unit). The self managed view has its own score.
    • Not included: furnishing or renovation, special assessments, depreciation or tax benefits. Taxes and depreciation are questions for a CPA. This is not tax or legal advice.

    Which listings are here

    • 401 active fee simple listings where nightly rental is legal: Waikiki resort zoned hotel condos and NUC units, Turtle Bay (Kuilima Estates TVU path and North Shore Club Residences), Ko Olina Beach Villas (Resort District, 6 night minimum), and the Cottages at Mauna Olu in Makaha (Resort District houses). 41 of them are at or under $3M with a positive all cash return and appear above. The rest lose money after costs at today's prices and are left off.
    • Leasehold listings are excluded completely. Everything shown is fee simple.
    • Apartment buildings (64 shown): every active fee simple 2+ unit building on Oahu up to $3M from the MLS multi-family sheets with a positive return. Rent is the seller's reported income when the MLS has it, otherwise HUD's FY2026 Fair Market Rent for Honolulu by bedroom (studio $1,877, 1 bd $2,016, 2 bd $2,642, 3 bd $3,674), reduced 10% to 20% for average or fair condition and 10% more for buildings built before 1960. Costs: 5% vacancy, 4.712% GET on rent, property tax by zoning (Residential A $4.00 / $11.40 above $1M, Apartment $3.50, commercial or industrial zoning $12.40) on the higher of assessed value or 90% of price, insurance $1,500 a unit, repairs 8%, reserves 5%, owner water and sewer $120 a unit, accounting, and an 8% manager. Buildings listed as needing major repair or tear down are left off, along with 1026 Mao Lane, where the unit count does not add up. Ask for a rent roll on any of them.
    • Budget: up to $3,000,000, no minimum. The two commercial buildings we found with a stated cap rate (401 Kamakee St and 624 Sheridan St) are above that and are not shown.
    • Luxury homes rented long term were not added. Above $1M they are taxed at $11.40 per $1,000 and typically return under 2% all cash.

    Before an offer

    • Confirm nightly use for the specific unit: resort zoning or a current NUC (renewed every year), the AOAO house rules, and city registration where it applies (Kuilima, Mauna Olu).
    • Cards marked “market average” assume the unit earns the area average. Strong photos, reviews and pricing can beat it, and a dated unit can fall short.
    • Hotel rental programs (Aqua-Aston, Ilikai, LXR, Ritz) usually keep 40% to 50% of gross, which is less than the 20% manager modeled here.
