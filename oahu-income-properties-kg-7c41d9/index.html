Good to know

How the returns work
  • Cash return (cap rate) = yearly net operating income ÷ price. With a loan: (NOI − mortgage) ÷ cash you put in (down payment + about 2% closing). DSCR = NOI ÷ mortgage; lenders usually want 1.20 to 1.25. Listings that lose money in your scenario are hidden.
  • Duplex to fourplex default 25% down, 7.75%, 30 years: Freddie Mac 30 year average 7.18% (Nov 5, 2026) plus an investment premium.
  • 5+ units, mixed use and commercial default 30% down, 7.75%, 25 year amortization: 10 year Treasury 4.98% (Nov 10, 2026) plus a typical 2.5 to 3 point commercial spread. Commercial loans often balloon in 5 to 10 years.
Where the income comes from
  • Listing-reported: the NOI, cap rate or rents stated in the MLS listing, with its basis. When there are several, the lower one is used. Sellers' figures often leave out vacancy, management or reserves, so ask for the rent roll and 24 months of statements.
  • Estimate: HUD 2026 Fair Market Rents for Urban Honolulu by bedroom ($1,877 studio, $2,016 one bd, $2,642 two bd, $3,674 three bd), lower for average or fair condition and pre 1960 buildings.
  • Estimate costs: 5% vacancy, GET 4.712%, property tax by zoning, insurance $1,500 a unit, repairs 8%, reserves 5%, utilities $120 a unit a month, accounting, 8% manager.

Tips for new investors
  • Compare cap rates first, then test the loan view. Many buildings only cash flow with a bigger down payment today.
  • Price per unit and rent per unit tell you more than total price.
  • Duplex to fourplex: living in one unit can open owner occupant loans. Ask a lender.
  • Check leases: month to month tenants, below market rents and who pays utilities change the real return.
  • Before an offer: rent roll, 24 months of statements, permits, zoning, inspection. Talk with a CPA and an attorney.

Good to know

What this page does and does not claim
  • This page lists only fee simple units where we found a written reason that short term rentals (under 90 days) are allowed: resort zoning from the MLS record, an MLS condo hotel flag, listing remarks, a known hotel rental program, or a Nonconforming Use Certificate (NUC) noted in earlier research. It is not a legal opinion and it is not verified in Matrix. Always confirm with the association and the City.
  • Leasehold units, parking stalls, and buildings where we could not find a reason that nightly rentals are allowed are left out. Units over about $482,000 cannot fit $130,000 of cash at 25% down, so they are not shown.
How the three net sheets work
  • Mortgage: 30 year fixed at 8.25% by default, 25% or 30% down, plus 2% of the price for closing costs. Condo hotel units are often non-warrantable, so they usually need a portfolio or DSCR loan with 25% to 35% down and a rate about 1 to 2 points above the owner occupied rate. 8.25% is our estimate, not a lender quote. Freddie Mac's 30 year average was 7.28% on Nov 19, 2026.
  • Cash needed = down payment + 2% closing. Lender reserve requirements, furnishing and any special assessment are extra and not counted.
  • Revenue is the gross room revenue before taxes. Base case: BNBCalc trailing 12 month average for the area and bedroom count (Nov 3, 2026), or seller-provided hotel owner statements for Ala Moana Hotel. The higher case adds 50%. Our Nov 19 research on Marine Surf Waikiki found public rate sources near $52,000 a year for a studio ($190 a night at 75% occupancy), well above the BNBCalc studio averages; that is why the higher case is offered. Nobody should rely on either number without 12 months of the seller's real statements.
  • Taxes on revenue: Hawaii GET 4.712% + state TAT 11% (since Jan 1, 2026) + Honolulu TAT 3% = 18.7% of gross. By default the owner absorbs all of it (the cautious view). Choose "Guests pay on top" to see the net if you charge it to guests. Hotel owner statements are already net of the hotel's taxes and share.
  • Self managed: 3% platform fee, 7% cleaning, linens and turnover shortfall, 5% reserve for furniture and upkeep. With a property manager: 20% by default (15% to 25% is typical; use the slider), plus 5% supplies and 5% reserve.
  • Hotel rental program: shown only where the building has one (Island Colony, Aloha Surf, Palms Waikiki, Waikiki Banyan, Ilikai, Ala Moana Hotel). The owner share is an assumption of 50% of room revenue after taxes, the usual condo hotel split described in academic research (MIT, Waikiki condo hotels). Actual splits are not published for most programs; Island Colony publishes only that profits after operating expenses and a 6% reserve go 99% to owners. Ask the operator for the last 12 months of owner statements. A listing does not say whether the unit is currently enrolled.
  • Ownership costs, all options: monthly maintenance (total monthly fees in the MLS); property tax at the Honolulu Transient Vacation rate of $9.00 per $1,000 of price ($13.90 per $1,000 for Ala Moana Hotel, hotel and resort class), a stand-in for the assessed value; $1,000 a year insurance for your own unit policy; $600 a year registration, license and accounting; and utilities ($1,440 electric, $600 water and sewer, $900 internet and cable a year) only when the MLS says the fee does not include them.
  • Break-even revenue is the gross revenue a unit needs each year for the option to net $0 a month after the mortgage.
