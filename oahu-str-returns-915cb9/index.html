How are you buying?

Pick one. Every number on the page updates.

Paying cash

You put in the full price and there is no mortgage. Your return is the yearly net income divided by the price, called the Cash Return (Cap Rate). Steady and simple, but all of your money is in one property.

Using a loan

You put in a down payment and the rent also has to cover the mortgage. We show cash on cash return (yearly cash flow after the mortgage divided by the cash you put in), monthly cash flow after the mortgage, and the cap rate for comparison. A loan can raise your return on the cash you put in when the property earns more than the loan costs, and lower it when it does not. At today's rates, a bigger down payment often makes the difference.

How the numbers work

Every figure here is an estimate for comparing properties, not a promise of income, appreciation or loan approval.

Income

  • Seller-reported: real income the seller provided, shown with its period. Ilikai #1518 uses the owner's Airbnb and VRBO payout statements (2025, the most recent and lowest year). Ala Moana Hotel units use the hotel's owner statements, showing what the owner actually received after the hotel program's share; when both a full year and a 2026 pace exist, the lower one is used because 2026 has been softer. Other seller figures come from the MLS listing and still need 24 months of statements to verify.
  • Estimate: the BNBCalc trailing 12 month average revenue for that area and bedroom count (data Nov 3, 2026). Great photos, reviews and pricing can beat it, and a dated unit can fall short.

Costs taken out

  • Professional manager 20% (switch to self managed to see the difference), cleaning per stay at about a 4 night average stay, HOA, property tax at the Hotel and Resort rate of $13.90 per $1,000 of assessed value, insurance, utilities when not in the HOA, supplies, 5% reserves, registration and accounting. Ala Moana Hotel units: hotel owner net minus HOA, property tax and insurance.
  • Hawaii GET and transient accommodations taxes are charged to guests on top of the nightly rate, so they are neither income nor cost here.
  • Not included: furnishing or renovation, special assessments, closing costs in the cash scenario, and any tax benefits.

Cash vs loan math

  • All cash: Cash Return (Cap Rate) = yearly net income ÷ price.
  • Loan: monthly principal and interest on the price minus your down payment at the rate and term you choose. Cash you put in = down payment + about 2% of the price for closing costs. Cash on cash = (yearly net income − yearly mortgage payments) ÷ cash you put in. Properties that would lose money in your scenario are hidden.
  • Default rate 7.75% = Freddie Mac's 30 year average of 7.18% (Nov 5, 2026) plus a typical premium for second home and investment loans. Your lender's quote will differ.
  • Est. total return. Cash: Cash Return + the area's 10 year yearly appreciation. Loan: (first year cash flow + first year principal paid down + one year of appreciation on the full price at the 10 year pace) ÷ cash you put in. Leverage magnifies appreciation in both directions.

Area appreciation (5 and 10 year, yearly)

  • Waikiki: 2.4% a year over 5 years, 2.3% a year over 10 years. Source: Waikiki condo median, Honolulu Board of REALTORS (2015 $349K, 2020 $390K, 2025 $440K).
  • Turtle Bay (North Shore): 8.0% a year over 5 years, 6.4% a year over 10 years. Source: North Shore region condo median, HBR (2015 $387.5K, 2020 $487.9K, 2025 $717.5K; small sample, about 36 to 72 sales a year).
  • Makaha: 4.9% a year over 5 years, 5.8% a year over 10 years. Source: Makaha to Nanakuli single family median, HBR (2015 $380K, 2020 $524K, 2025 $665K).
  • Ko Olina: 4.0% a year over 5 years, 4.7% a year over 10 years. Source: Ewa Plain condo median, HBR (2015 $409K, 2020 $530K, 2025 $645K).
  • Ala Moana: 3.1% a year over 5 years, 3.5% a year over 10 years. Source: Oahu wide condo median, HBR (2015 $360K, 2020 $435K, 2025 $507.3K). The Ala Moana to Kakaako median is skewed by new luxury towers, so the island figure is used.
  • Medians are full-year resale medians from the Honolulu Board of REALTORS® Local Market Updates for 2015, 2020 and 2025. Past appreciation does not predict future prices.

Before an offer

  • Confirm nightly use for the specific unit: resort zoning or a current NUC (renewed every year), the AOAO rules, any hotel rental program terms, and city registration where it applies.
  • Get the seller's statements for at least 24 months.
  • Talk with a CPA about taxes and depreciation and an attorney about legal questions. Nothing here is tax, legal or lending advice.
